## Open Sound Meter Highlights

- **Level In The Room:** Open Sound Meter SPL meter shows A or C weighted level from a mic you already calibrated, which is the question a handheld meter used to answer.
- **Bars While Sound Happens:** Open Sound Meter RTA draws a live spectrum so a hum or a ring in a space you control is visible without waiting for a stored sweep.
- **Numbers You Can Trust After Cal:** Open Sound Meter level display is only as honest as the sensitivity you enter for the capsule you keep.
- **Laptop Instead Of A Drawer Of Meters:** Open Sound Meter acoustic meter sits on an interface you already own, useful when a second hardware SPL box is not in the bag.
- **Not A Transfer-Function Desk:** There is no dual-FFT engine here. Alignment of arrays still belongs in a different program.

## What Is Open Sound Meter

Open Sound Meter is a Windows readout for people who already carry a measurement microphone and want SPL plus a spectrum. You patch the mic, set calibration, and watch Open Sound Meter SPL meter while a band plays or a generator runs. Open Sound Meter RTA is the bar graph. Open Sound Meter level display is the big number. Open Sound Meter acoustic meter is the whole habit: look at the room you are standing in, not at a saved EQ from last year.

System techs reach for this when Smaart would be more program than the question. “How loud is FOH?” and “is that 250 Hz bump real?” do not always need coherence plots. A teacher checking a gym you administer, a club owner glancing at a legal limit, or a rehearsal space you control can use the same seat.

It will not invent a calibration you did not load, and it will not time-align subs. Work in rooms you are allowed to measure. Keep the mic file with the laptop. A screenshot from someone else’s gig is not your meter.

## Starting with Open Sound Meter

Prerequisites: A PC you administer, a measurement mic with a known sensitivity, and a room you are allowed to check.

1. **Prepare:** Mount the capsule at the seat you care about and confirm the interface input is the one you intend.
2. **Configure:** Enter calibration so Open Sound Meter SPL meter matches a handheld you trust, if you have one.
3. **Test:** Play a known source and watch Open Sound Meter level display; a mute or a wrong input shows up immediately.
4. **Refine:** Set weighting and Open Sound Meter RTA bands for the question of this session, not every option at once.
5. **Apply:** Read levels during soundcheck or class in the space you control.
6. **Maintain:** Store the cal number with that capsule, and do not borrow a sensitivity from a forum.
